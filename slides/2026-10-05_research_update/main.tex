\section{The question}

\begin{frame}{AI can now generate video that never ends}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.40\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
  \item New \emph{streaming} video models write a video one short chunk at a time, each chunk looking at the last few.
  \item In principle they can run forever: games, live worlds, endless scenes.
  \item We ran three of them for \textbf{3 minutes} on 8 very different prompts.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{4pt}
\take{What happens to a video\\ if you just keep going?}
\column{0.58\textwidth}
\centering
\fig{photo_end_states_top}\\[-1pt]
{\scriptsize The first frame of each of the 8 prompts (Self Forcing).}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{\ldots but on Self Forcing, they all die the same death}
\centering
\figh{0.66}{photo_sf_converge}\\[2pt]
{\scriptsize One model (Self Forcing), six prompts, frames from 0 to 178 s. Mammoths, waves, a monster, a pigeon, pirate ships: after three minutes they all become the same purple-blue streaks.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The question, in one sentence}
\take{\large Long videos \emph{decay}. \textbf{Where} do they go, \textbf{how fast}, and \textbf{which design choice} decides it?}
\vspace{-6pt}
\centering
\figh{0.56}{line_phase_portrait}\\[-1pt]
{\scriptsize Frame-by-frame trajectories of generated videos, one line per video, colour = prompt. A marble dropped on a landscape rolls into a valley, an \emph{attractor}. Self Forcing's videos all roll into one valley; the other models' videos each stay in their own. The 2-D map keeps 19\% of the variation, so slide 13 measures distances in the full embedding.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why this matters}
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{block}{A common problem, with very different outcomes}
\footnotesize Streaming video, game engines and world models generate one step at a time from their own output. Long rollouts often accumulate drift, and recent designs (sinks, long-video tuning) reduce it a lot. In our runs, how a video ends depends strongly on the model and its inference settings.
\end{block}
\begin{block}{Measured as symptoms, not as dynamics}
\footnotesize Benchmarks and recent papers already quantify degradation: quality and consistency over long videos (VBench-Long), colour and motion drift, spectral drift, representation collapse, sink collapse. What we have not found is a characterization of the \emph{dynamics}: where trajectories converge, how fast the prompt is lost, how perturbations grow, which design change alters the end state.
\end{block}
\column{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{block}{What we provide}
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item A map of each model's \textbf{end states}
  \item A \textbf{memory half-life} for the prompt
  \item \textbf{Sensitivity}: does a tiny nudge change the story?
  \item \textbf{Tipping knobs}: one design change flips the end state
  \item Tools anyone can run on a new model
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\section{How we got here}

\begin{frame}{Where we started: fixing videos at test time}
\begin{columns}[c]
\column{0.38\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}\small
  \item Original project: adapt the model to each video while generating (\emph{test-time adaptation}, TTA).
  \item At 1{,}000-video scale it does \textbf{nothing measurable}: FVD 154.7 $\to$ 153.4, within noise.

\section{Approach}

\begin{frame}{Setup}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Models (all on Wan2.1, 1.3B)}
\begin{itemize}\small
  \item \textbf{Self Forcing}: rolling window, no anchor
  \item \textbf{Rolling Forcing}: keeps the first chunk as an anchor (``sink'')
  \item \textbf{LongLive}: sink plus long-video tuning
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\begin{block}{Rollouts}
\small 8 MovieGen prompts $\times$ 2 seeds $\times$ 3 models = 48 videos of 3 minutes (2{,}877 frames each). Plus 128 real 30\,s clips as a control. About 8 H200-hours so far.
\end{block}
\column{0.50\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Measurements, every frame}
\begin{itemize}\small
  \item \textbf{Content}: DINOv2 embedding (what is in the frame)
  \item \textbf{Look}: brightness, contrast, saturation, colourfulness, sharpness, spectral slope
  \item \textbf{Motion}: frame difference, optical flow
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\begin{block}{Treat the generator as a dynamical system}
\small The state is the recent frames. The rule is the model. The noise is the random seed.
\end{block}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Toolkit: dynamical-systems questions, video measurements}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\bfseries}l X X}
\toprule
Concept & Question & Measurement \\
\midrule
Attractor & Where do videos end up? & Distance between videos of different prompts; can the prompt still be identified from the frame? \\
Relaxation & How fast? & Exponential fit to that distance; prompt \emph{half-life} \\
Lyapunov (twins) & Does a tiny nudge change the story? & Re-run with one noise block perturbed; track how fast the twins separate \\
Bifurcation & Which knob flips the end state? & Same weights, change sink size or attention window \\
Recurrence & Is the motion structured or random? & Recurrence analysis vs.\ phase-randomized surrogates \\
Early warning & Can we see collapse coming? & Rising variance and autocorrelation before collapse \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Self Forcing has one global attractor}
\centering
\figh{0.42}{line_convergence}\\[2pt]
{\scriptsize (a) Content distance between two videos at each second (1 $-$ cosine similarity of their DINOv2 embeddings; 0 = identical content, 1 = unrelated). Solid: pairs with different prompts. Dashed: same prompt, different seed, the spread due to randomness alone. (b) For each video and second, we find the most similar video from the other seed at the same second; the share of matches with the correct prompt (chance = 1 in 8).}
\take{Self Forcing: distance falls 0.97 $\to$ 0.64 and identification falls 100\% $\to$ 34\%. Rolling Forcing and LongLive keep every prompt (100\% and 98\%). This is not shared noise: the seeds differ.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The prompt has a half-life of about one minute}
\begin{columns}[c]
\column{0.60\textwidth}
\fig{line_halflife}
\column{0.38\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}\small
  \item Distance between different prompts decays exponentially: half-life \textbf{about 1 minute} (58--66\,s depending on the fit window).
  \item It levels off at the \textbf{noise floor}: two seeds of the \emph{same} prompt are as far apart as two different prompts. The prompt is fully erased.
  \item Pull toward the end state is exponential too: $\tau \approx 360$\,s, $R^2 = 0.91$.
\end{itemize}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What the decay looks like}
\centering
\figh{0.56}{line_observables}\\[2pt]
{\scriptsize Low-level measurements per frame, averaged per second, for the 16 videos of each model (8 prompts $\times$ 2 seeds).}
\take{Self Forcing gets darker, more saturated and streaky. Rolling Forcing brightens toward glare. LongLive holds its look. Motion collapses fast (seconds); appearance drifts slowly (minutes).}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Twins: same video, one tiny nudge}
\centering
\figh{0.38}{photo_twins_sf}\\[4pt]
{\scriptsize Self Forcing, ``Tokyo street''. The runs are bit-for-bit reproducible, so the only difference between the two rows is one block of input noise nudged at 29\,s. Identical at 20 and 30\,s, slightly different by 60\,s, telling different stories by 120\,s.}
\take{Does a tiny nudge change the story? For Self Forcing, yes.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Twins: sensitive is not the same as unstable}
\centering
\figh{0.54}{line_twins}\\[2pt]
{\scriptsize All 8 prompts per model, seed 0. $\varepsilon$ = relative size of the nudge to one noise block. (a) Content distance between twins over time, log scale; the twins are identical before 29\,s. (b) How fast the gap grows in the model's latent space in the first 7.5\,s: the same rate for both nudge sizes. (c) Content gap in the last 10\,s.}
\take{LongLive is the \emph{most} sensitive, yet its twins end as close as Rolling Forcing's. Only Self Forcing lets a tiny nudge change the story.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One knob flips the attractor}
\centering
\figh{0.52}{photo_bifurcation_p0}\\[3pt]
{\scriptsize Same Self Forcing weights, same prompt and seed; only two inference settings change, both counted in latent frames (1 latent frame = 4 video frames = 0.25\,s). \emph{Window}: how far back the model attends (21 $\approx$ 5.3\,s, 12 = 3\,s). \emph{Sink}: the first latent frames kept in memory permanently (3 = the first 0.75\,s).}
\take{A sink of 3 latent frames keeps the scene alive for 3 minutes. A shorter window alone still collapses, just into a different pattern.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One knob flips the attractor: all 8 prompts}
\centering
\figh{0.54}{line_bifurcation}\\[2pt]
{\scriptsize Same four settings, averaged over all 8 prompts. (a) Content distance between videos of different prompts. (b) How similar each video stays to its own first 2\,s.}
\take{Without a sink, prompts merge and openings are forgotten (distance $\to$ 0.67, similarity $\to$ 0.2). With a sink, prompts stay apart ($>$0.9) and openings are kept ($\approx$0.7). One knob switches a \emph{global} attractor into \emph{prompt-anchored} ones: a bifurcation.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Collapse is a slow ramp, with no early warning}
\centering
\figh{0.53}{line_ews}\\[2pt]
{\scriptsize (a) Each line is one Self Forcing video, shifted so its collapse (the first second after which it stays closer to the shared end state than to its own opening for 10\,s) sits at 0. Going from 10\% to 90\% of the way takes about 90\,s. (b) Share of videos whose lag-1 autocorrelation rises during the 60\,s before collapse (blue) versus the same window in models that never collapse (grey). A real early warning would make blue far higher.}
\take{Collapse is a slow ramp, not a tipping point. That is a negative result we will report.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The motion is structured, not noise}
\begin{columns}[c]
\column{0.55\textwidth}
\figh{0.62}{bar_rqa}
\column{0.43\textwidth}
\small
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Recurrence analysis} asks how often a video revisits an earlier state, and whether those revisits repeat as whole sequences, like a pendulum swinging back.
  \item We compare each rollout with a copy that keeps its frequency content but scrambles its timing (phase randomization).
  \item Every model is more structured than its scrambled copy, in 100\% of runs.
\end{itemize}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Checking a rival claim: periodic ``snap-back''}
\centering
\figh{0.36}{line_lol}\\[2pt]
{\scriptsize LoL (arXiv 2601.16914) reports LongLive snapping back to its first frames at the same moments in every video. For each video we track sudden rises in similarity to its own first second, average them across the 16 videos of a model, and take the strongest moment (red line). Histogram: the same statistic after shifting each video randomly in time, which destroys any shared timing (500 times).}
\take{No synchronized snap-back within 3 minutes for any model ($p \geq 0.8$). LoL studies hour-long videos, so a longer LongLive run is still open.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Has anyone done this already?}
\small
About 60 targeted searches (2024 to Oct 2026), rechecked Oct 6. Long-horizon degradation is \textbf{measured widely}. What we did not find is any work that treats streaming video generators as \textbf{dynamical systems}.
\vspace{2pt}
\scriptsize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.08}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\linewidth} X >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.24\linewidth}}
\toprule
Closest work & What it shows & Relation to ours \\
\midrule
VBench-Long, Relax Forcing, In-Distribution Forcing (2610.03120) & Quality, consistency, colour and motion drift over minutes & measure symptoms; we model dynamics \\
LoL (2601.16914) & Sink-collapse loops at hour scale & partial: test longer \\
Deep / Rolling Sink, FreqForcing (2607.27110) & A sink slows drift; drift as spectral-energy shift & partial: we show a \emph{type switch} \\
Effective-rank collapse (2607.27036) & Abrupt representation collapse at drift onset & reconcile with ``slow ramp'' \\
Meta-ARVDM (2503.10704) & Theory of history forgetting & frames, not the prompt \\
Biroli et al., Nat.\ Commun.\ 2024 & Attractor-like regimes \emph{inside} the denoising steps & other time axis \\
Hintze et al., Patterns 2025 & Iterated image loops collapse to $\sim$12 attractors & concept, not video \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\vspace{1pt}
\take{\footnotesize Safe claim: existing work measures long-horizon degradation and specific failure modes; we characterize where trajectories converge, how fast prompt information is lost, how perturbations grow, and which inference settings change the attractor. Avoid: ``nobody has measured it'', ``every model collapses'', ``sinks prevent drift'' (known).}
\end{frame}

  \item Release the toolkit as a drop-in diagnostic
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\take{\textbf{Takeaway.} Long videos do not just ``get worse''. Each model falls into its own kind of attractor, at a measurable rate, and a single design knob can change which kind.}
